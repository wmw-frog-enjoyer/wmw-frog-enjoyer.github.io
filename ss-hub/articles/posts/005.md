# The Comfort Zone Loop: How Different Types of Players Play Different Types of Maps

I feel like most players have a subset of maps that they play most (the comfort maps you don't really need to think about or that you just have fun playing). You can just pop them up and have a good time. For me, Sound Space is a comfort game. It is the type of game I open when I am bored and I don't really feel like exploring much more than what I have already done. Because of this, I do have a set of maps I have favorited over time, and I never really explore much outside of those maps.

## Understanding the Comfort Map-Set

Just like I have my personal map set, a lot of other players do too. We try new maps out and like some so much we keep revisiting them. Over time (maybe months, maybe years) we make a map list of things we really enjoy playing. This comfort happens because all players have different preferences, not just on straightforward things like their favorite patterns, but also on more specific and personal things. This includes liking the way a certain mapper represents a song, or feeling like a specific song had to be represented a certain way, and after a while, you find a map that does exactly that. For whatever reason, players tend to like certain maps a lot, and so, we revisit them. We do this so much and with so many maps that, at some point, we have a list of maps that we are very comfortable playing. We don't feel like changing that, and we see no point in doing so.

As a side note to this, I believe newer players can't really relate to this feeling yet. From what I have seen, new players try anything out; they don't have set-in-stone preferences, and they don't quite know when a map is properly representing a song or not. What they like or dislike on maps is simply based on their small feeling of the game. While most of my texts are for beginners, I unfortunately think this is one of the topics newer players will have more trouble relating to.

## Categorizing Player Types and Their Preferences

To understand how these preferences form, I enjoy separating players into categories. While I think people are too complex to be separated into little boxes, it makes it much easier to represent different playstyles. Depending on where you are on your Sound Space adventure, the way you interact with that comfort map set is going to be different:

- **Inactive Players:** One of my favorite types of players, and the type I currently am, is the inactive player; these are people who play occasionally to throw some stress off. These players have played for a good while, so they know their preferences and likely already have a set of maps that they really enjoy playing. Because we play so little, spending time looking for new maps to play seems a bit pointless. It's less likely we will get tired of the maps we already have saved, and everyone hates going through tons of bad maps before finding a new good one.
    
    These types of players also tend to like simpler patterns. Relearning how to play the game is already annoying enough; I don't want to be stressing myself even more trying to relearn how to play tech maps. I personally enjoy doing very basic jumps or slides and avoid anything that makes me actually need to focus on the game.
    
- **Returning Players:** Another category of players that is somewhat similar to the old inactive players is returning players. Much like the other OGs, they too have a vast list of good maps they already know and can pick from, though these players are more likely to open up to play recommendations from other people, and they might even try some techier maps.
    
- **Players at Peak Performance:** The players at their peak, on the other hand, can be separated into two little groups. The first group is actively exploring and finding new maps, setting great passes on them. The other group is playing the same set of maps they were playing three years prior, trying to increase their accuracy or speed.
    

I think I could spend an entire day writing about how these different types of players like different types of maps, but I think my message is clear.

## The Long-Term Impact of Comfort Maps

Looking at the player types I just mentioned, I think the OG inactive player is the only one that does not experience major downsides from having a comfort map set. Those players don't really gain much from learning anything new; it is not like they will be playing a lot anyway. It doesn't really make a lot of sense for them to be playing bad maps when they don't even play all that much. They might like maps they get recommended, but most of those OG players just want to play the maps they did years back. It isn't only about gameplay; it's about coming back to the memories we had of Sound Space. That, too, makes playing the game fun.

But when an OG player is coming back to stay, staying on the same old set of maps can negatively impact them. For starters, the "newgens" are going to be playing different things, so it will be harder for the old player to relate to the new active community. If they are actually going to play anyway, they might as well discover new things they like. They need to learn new things and get used to playing the game again, so trying out new maps can be highly beneficial.

For players at peak performance, I can see the argument for why they stay on a certain set of maps. It makes it easier to contrast your current performance with what you were before, and your growth becomes clearer. However, playing other maps gets you used to how other mappers represent different songs. Those skills can help boost your ability in general.

Just as I think a peak player who only plays old maps isn't fully using their abilities, I also don't think players who try only new maps are totally in the right either. If you don't have a set of maps that you are comfortable with and play a lot, it will become way harder for you to compare your performance to how you were doing a couple of months back. When you play a certain set of maps a lot, you create a benchmark you can look back on in the future.

## Balancing the Old and the New

Ultimately, having a comfortable map set isn't all bad, but you shouldn't over-rely on those comfortable picks and stop playing anything new. Any excess is bad, and just as playing only old maps can be bad, only playing new ones can also cause issues. It's all about finding good maps to play in the long run but not limiting yourself to them, because when you do, you stop giving yourself space to grow.
